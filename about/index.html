I'm Kristi — a software developer in Scottsdale, AZ with a path here that took a few interesting detours.

I started out studying neurobiology and physiology at Purdue, fascinated by how complex systems work at a fundamental level. That curiosity led me to nursing school at ASU, and then to five years as a registered nurse, most recently in the Cardiac Catheterization Lab at HonorHealth, where I helped perform interventional heart procedures in a fast-paced, high-stakes environment. I cross-trained in every role and for multiple procedure types. I could scrub and circulate (though not at the same time!) cardiac caths, LVAD implantation, pacemakers, structural heart. I loved the problem-solving, the teamwork, and the impact I knew we had each and every day.

Life in healthcare was exciting and intense! I got to work a lot of muscles doing work that required intellect, emotional intelligence and physical dexterity and endurance. It's no small feat running around in a suit of lead for a 12-hour day, especially if you have to be emotionally present and reassuring for your patient and their family, have tools or meds ready for a physician as soon as they realize they need it (though, if you had it before they realized they needed it would be best!) and be quick to catch, assess and accurately intervene for critical changes in patient status at a moment's notice. Not to mention on-call hours attached, so that 12-hour day could turn into 14, or 16...

The work was rewarding, but the intensity is high and the impact is relative narrow. One patient at a time. When I was ready to look for something that was a little more sustainable, I went back to what was my initial hook. Figuring out how systems fit together. Making changes that make things better. I was ready to debug not just equipment or vital signs, but systems. So I made the leap.

I completed an intensive full-stack bootcamp, then was selected for the software apprenticeship at Clean Coders Studio, where I learned under the mentorship of experienced developers, including Robert C. Martin (Uncle Bob). That experience shaped how I think about code: as a craft. I believe in test-driven development, SOLID principles, and writing code that the next person can actually read. I got promoted to Software Craftsman and spent the rest of my time there contributing production code across the full stack.

These days I'm exploring, but I'm always picking up something new — the blog started during my apprenticeship at clean coders, but has continued with my learning journey as a place where I think out loud about whatever I'm learning.

What I bring from nursing into software is more than I expected: the ability to stay calm when things break, to collaborate across disciplines, to ask good questions, and to care deeply about getting the details right.

When I'm not coding, I'm probably reading, hiking, playing a board game with friends convincing my garden not to die in the heat.

Cheers!
